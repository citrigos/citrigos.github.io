Department of Demography | University of California, Berkeley

I am a second-year PhD student in Demography at UC Berkeley. Taking a life course perspective, I study how social structures shape how families form and dissolve and what gets carried into the next generation. I am particularly interested in immigrant families, where structural pressures and cultural negotiations make visible processes that may go unobserved in the majority population. My current work focuses on how these families form under pressure, what holds them together, and what gets carried forward.

My path to demography has been unconventional, bringing a technical and data-focused approach to my work. I studied Mathematics with Computer Science at MIT and worked as a software engineer at Capital One before pivoting to population research. I hold an M.S. in Data Science from the University of Helsinki (2024), an M.A. in Demography from UC Berkeley (2026), and completed the European Doctoral School of Demography (EDSD), where I trained at the Max Planck Institute for Demographic Research in Rostock and INED in Paris between 2024–2025.
