Research & Presentations

My research asks how social structures shape family life across the life course and what gets carried into the next generation. I am particularly interested in immigrant families, where structural pressures and cultural negotiations make visible processes that may go unobserved in the majority population. Current projects examine union formation and dissolution, fertility, and mental health in the transition to parenthood. Each approaches the family as both a site where constraint and advantage are experienced and a mechanism through which they are reproduced.

How does immigration background shape the way families form and dissolve?

Union formation and dissolution are sensitive to social context — norms, opportunity structures, partner markets, and cultural expectations all play a role. Using Finnish register data, I study how immigration generation shapes the timing and likelihood of entering and exiting first union, and how the combination of each partner's background matters.

Trigos-Raczkowski C & Wright KQ (co-first authors), Martikainen P, Pitkänen J, Moustgaard H, & Myrskylä M. “Contribution of a Couple’s Immigration Status to Union Formation and Dissolution in Finland.” Under Review.

How do immigrant women and their descendants contribute to fertility in low-fertility contexts?

Immigrant women and their descendants make up a growing share of the reproductive-age population in Finland. This project examines how first birth patterns and fertility rates vary across immigrant generations, and what this means for the broader fertility regime.

Wright KQ, Trigos-Raczkowski C, Loi S, Pitkänen J, Myrskylä M & Martikainen P. “The Contributions of the Fertility of Women with Immigrant Backgrounds to the Low Fertility Regime in Finland.”

How does immigration background shape mental health across the transition to parenthood?

The transition to parenthood marks a period of elevated mental health risk — but healthcare use doesn't map neatly onto need, and inequitable access to care is itself a meaningful outcome. Using Finnish administrative data on over 420,000 first-time mothers, I examine patterns of mental healthcare use before and after childbirth across immigrant generations, and how structural advantage and disadvantage shape who accesses care.

Trigos-Raczkowski C, Loi S, Wright KQ, Pitkänen J, Martikainen P, & Myrskylä M. “Mental Healthcare Utilization in the Transition to Motherhood among Immigrants and their Descendants in Finland.”
